Question: What are the key differences between a Health Savings Account (HSA) and a Flexible Spending Account (FSA)?

Answer: Both Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) and Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs) allow you to pay for qualified medical expenses using pre-tax dollars, reducing your taxable income. However, they differ substantially in plan eligibility, ownership, rollover rules, and contribution limits:

1. Plan Eligibility:
- HSA: Requires enrollment in a qualified High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP), such as the Apex Bronze HDHP Plan. You cannot be covered under any non-HDHP health insurance or enrolled in Medicare.
- Healthcare FSA: Can be paired with any health insurance plan tier (including HMO, PPO, or HDHP) and is sponsored directly through your employer.

2. Ownership and Portability:
- HSA: The account is owned entirely by you. It is fully portable, meaning the funds remain yours indefinitely even if you change employers, change health plans, or retire.
- FSA: The account is owned by your employer. If you leave your company, unspent funds are generally forfeited to the employer.

3. Rollover Rules:
- HSA: Unused balances roll over 100% year after year with no expiration. Account balances can be invested in mutual funds, stocks, and bonds, growing tax-free.
- FSA: Governed by the 'use-it-or-lose-it' rule. Under IRS regulations, employers may offer either a 2.5-month grace period to spend remaining funds or permit a rollover of up to $640 into the subsequent plan year. Any excess unspent balance is forfeited.

4. Annual Contribution Limits (2026 Tax Year):
- HSA: Up to $4,300 for individual coverage; up to $8,550 for family coverage. Individuals aged 55 and older can make an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution.
- FSA: Up to $3,200 per employee.
