This document details federal and contractual restrictions on balance billing, outlining protections established under the federal No Surprises Act (Public Law 116-260) for covered members.

Definition of Balance Billing:
Balance billing occurs when an out-of-network healthcare provider bills a patient for the difference between the provider's gross billed charge and the insurance plan's Allowed Amount. For example, if a non-participating physician bills $1,500 for a service and the plan's allowable amount is $700, balance billing represents the remaining $800 balance.

In-Network Prohibition:
Participating in-network healthcare providers are contractually prohibited from balance billing members. Contracted providers must accept the negotiated Allowed Amount as payment in full and must write off all contractual discounts.

Federal No Surprises Act Protections:
Federal law strictly prohibits balance billing and surprise medical bills in the following specific circumstances:
1. Emergency Services:
Emergency services rendered by out-of-network emergency departments, freestanding emergency facilities, and out-of-network physicians (including emergency physicians and on-call specialists) cannot be balance billed. Member cost-sharing must be calculated at the in-network benefit level and must count toward in-network deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums.

2. Non-Emergency Services at In-Network Facilities:
When a member receives non-emergency care at an in-network hospital or ambulatory surgical center, non-participating ancillary providers (such as anesthesiologists, radiologists, pathologists, and assistant surgeons) cannot balance bill the patient unless the patient gave prior written consent under strict notice-and-consent rules. Ancillary providers are prohibited from seeking consent waivers.

3. Air Ambulance Services:
Out-of-network air ambulance providers cannot balance bill members.

Dispute Resolution:
Payment disputes between Apex Health Plans and out-of-network providers for protected surprise billing services are resolved through the Federal Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR) arbitration process, holding the member harmless from excess costs.
