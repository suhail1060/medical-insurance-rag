Question: What is COBRA health insurance coverage, how much does it cost, and what are the rules for electing it after job termination?

Answer: The Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (COBRA) is a federal law that allows employees and their covered dependents to temporarily maintain their existing group health insurance coverage following certain qualifying life events, such as voluntary or involuntary job termination or a reduction in work hours.

Key Provisions of COBRA Coverage:
1. Identical Coverage:
COBRA is not a separate health insurance plan. It provides the exact same network, deductibles, copayments, and prescription drug formularies you had under your employer-sponsored plan (e.g., Apex PPO Gold Plan). Deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums accumulated prior to the qualifying event carry over into your COBRA coverage for the remainder of the calendar year.

2. Premium Costs and Administrative Fees:
Under active employment, your employer typically subsidizes a substantial portion of your monthly premium. Under COBRA, you are responsible for paying the entire monthly premium (both the employee portion and the former employer portion), plus an allowable 2% administrative fee. As a result, you pay 102% of the total plan cost. For example, if your employee payroll deduction was $150 and your employer contributed $450, your monthly COBRA premium will be $612 ($600 total premium + $12 fee).

3. Election Timeline:
You have an election window of sixty (60) days from the date of your qualifying event or the date your COBRA election notice is mailed, whichever is later. Coverage is retroactive to the date group coverage ended, meaning you must pay all retroactive premiums from that date forward.

4. Duration:
COBRA continuation coverage typically lasts up to eighteen (18) months for standard job termination or reduction in hours, and up to thirty-six (36) months for covered dependents in cases of divorce, legal separation, or death of the primary employee.
